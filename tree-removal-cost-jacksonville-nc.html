How much does tree removal cost in Jacksonville, NC?

Honest, typical price ranges for tree work in coastal North Carolina — and what makes your price land where it does. These are ranges, not quotes.

The honest answer first

Nobody can give you an exact price for a tree they haven’t seen — not us, and not any honest company. A 20-foot pine in an open front yard and a 20-foot pine leaning over your roof are completely different jobs. What we can give you are the rough, typical ranges that tree work usually falls into, so you can tell a fair quote from an outlier.

Treat every number below as a starting point for your expectations, not a promise. A provider gives the real number only after seeing the tree and the property.

Typical cost ranges

Small tree removal (under ~30 ft)

Roughly $200–$600. Small ornamentals or young pines with easy access and nothing nearby to hit. The simplest removals land here.

Medium tree removal (~30–60 ft)

Roughly $600–$1,200. The most common Jacksonville job: a mature loblolly pine or oak that needs sectional takedown near a house, fence, or driveway.

Large tree removal (60 ft and up)

Roughly $1,200–$2,500+, with complex jobs going higher. Big, hazardous, or hard-to-reach trees — especially ones needing a crane — cost the most.

Trimming & pruning

Roughly $200–$800 per tree. Light shaping costs less; raising a canopy or clearing limbs off a roofline costs more. Whole-property pruning is usually quoted per project.

Stump grinding

Roughly $100–$400 per stump, depending on diameter and how deep you want it ground. Often quoted as an add-on to a removal.

Brush & lot clearing

Varies widely. Clearing saplings and brush from a side lot is priced per project — size of the area and how tangled it is decide the number.

What moves the price

Why two similar trees can cost very different amounts

Size and condition

Taller trees with thicker trunks take more time, more crew, and more equipment. Dead, hollow, or storm-damaged trees are unpredictable to climb and cut, which adds risk — and cost.

Location and access

A pine in an open front yard is straightforward. The same pine in a fenced backyard with no equipment access, overhanging the house, or tangled with power lines is a slower, more careful — and pricier — job.

Crane vs. climb

When a tree can’t be felled in one piece or safely climbed, crews bring a crane. That solves hard problems, but crane time is one of the biggest single cost drivers on a quote.

Cleanup and haul-away

Some quotes include full cleanup and hauling the wood away; others leave the logs and grind only the stump. Always ask what “done” includes before you compare two prices.

Timing and urgency

After a hurricane or tropical storm, demand spikes across Onslow County and urgent scheduling can cost more. Routine, scheduled work in a calm week is almost always the better value.

Before you request

Getting a quote you can trust

A real quote comes from a provider who has seen the tree — in person or from good photos and a clear description. When the quote arrives, get it in writing: what work is included, whether cleanup and haul-away are covered, and whether stump grinding is separate. Be cautious of anyone demanding full payment before the work starts.

Frequently asked questions

Why can’t I get an exact tree removal price online?

No one can price a tree they haven’t seen. Height, trunk diameter, lean, nearby structures, and access all change the job — a provider can only give a real quote after assessing the property.

Does sending a request through this site cost anything?

No. This is a free request resource run by New River Digital. Sending a request costs nothing and doesn’t commit you to any work.

Will a provider’s quote match these ranges?

It may land above or below. These are rough, typical ranges — your tree’s size, condition, and location decide the actual number. Always get the provider’s quote in writing before work begins.

Does urgent or storm work cost more?

Often, yes — urgent scheduling, hazardous conditions, and high demand after storms can raise prices. For immediate danger or downed power lines, call 911 or your utility first; this site doesn’t provide emergency dispatch.
