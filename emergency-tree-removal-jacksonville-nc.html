Urgent Tree Situation in Jacksonville, NC — Read This First

If there is immediate danger, call 911 now

Be blunt for a moment, because this matters: this site is a free request resource run by New River Digital. We are not a tree company, and we are not an emergency line. The phone number below is not an emergency line either. If a tree is on a house, on a car, blocking a road, or touching a power line, call 911 or your utility company right now — do not fill out a form, do not wait for a callback.

For everything short of immediate danger — a tree that came down in the yard overnight, a pine leaning hard after a storm, limbs hanging over the driveway — the next steps matter, and doing them in the right order keeps everyone safe. That’s what this page covers: the first ten minutes, then how a free request works once the situation is stable.

The first ten minutes

Get clear.

Move people, pets, and cars away from the tree and from where it could fall or roll. A fallen tree can shift, and leaning trees can come down without warning.

Look for lines.

If any part of the tree is on, under, or near a power line, stay far away and call your utility or 911. Never try to move a branch off a line yourself.

Don’t climb or cut.

Storm-damaged trees are under tension and behave unpredictably. This is the single most dangerous time to pick up a chainsaw — leave it to people with the equipment and insurance for it.

Document from safety.

Photos from a safe distance help everyone later — your insurance, and any provider. Take them now; you don’t need to go back.

Check structures.

If the tree touched the house, check for broken windows, damaged siding, or a shifted roofline — then call your insurance company. We can’t help with claims, but photos and a timeline help yours.

Then send a request.

Once the situation is stable and there’s no immediate danger, send us a free request describing what happened. We’ll help you frame the next steps — and if it’s truly an emergency still, we’ll tell you to call 911 again.

Frequently asked questions

Are you a tree company or an emergency service?

Neither. This is a free, independent request resource run by New River Digital — we are not a tree company and we do not provide emergency dispatch. For immediate danger, call 911 or your utility company.

A tree is on my house / on a car / blocking the road. What do I do?

Call 911 right now. Do not touch the tree, do not stand near it, and do not wait on a form or a phone line. Send a request only after the immediate danger is handled.

A tree is leaning badly after the storm but hasn’t fallen. What do I do?

Stay well away from it, keep people and pets clear, and treat it as a possible fall zone. You can send us a request describing the lean — we’ll help you frame it for a provider — but anything threatening a structure or blocking access should go to 911 first.

There are branches on a power line.

Stay clear and call your utility company or 911. No tree service — and not us — should touch anything on a line.

Does it cost anything to send a request?

No. It’s free, and it doesn’t commit you to any work.

Will my information be shared?

We use your details to follow up about your request, and we’ll tell you before sharing them with any provider.
